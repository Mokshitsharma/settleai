# Conversation guide (10 to 15 minutes)

**Before you start, say:** "I'm building a small project for a hackathon about paying for deliveries and small jobs safely.
Can I ask you a few questions? I won't use your name. Is it OK if I note down what you say, and quote a short line without
your name?" Record the answer to the consent question in the notes. If they say no to quoting, you can still use what they
said as a pattern, but not as a quote.

Ask about real past events, not opinions about the product. "Tell me about the last time..." beats "Would you use...?".
Don't pitch SettleAI until the last question.

## For delivery drivers and couriers

1. What kind of deliveries or jobs do you do, and who pays you? (platform, shop directly, cash, UPI, other)
2. Tell me about the last time a payment was late, short, or never came. What happened, and what did you do?
3. How long do you usually wait to be paid after finishing a job?
4. Has anyone ever said the job wasn't done properly to avoid paying? How did that end?
5. What proof do you keep that you delivered? (photos, signatures, messages)
6. If the money was held safely before you started, and released when your photo and location proved delivery, would that
   change anything for you? What would worry you about it?

## For shopkeepers and small businesses

1. What do you pay others to deliver or do for you, and how often?
2. How do you pay: before the work, after, part and part? Why that way?
3. Tell me about the last time something went wrong: goods damaged, late, not delivered, or a dispute about it.
4. How did it get settled? Who lost money?
5. Would you let an app or an AI assistant book deliveries for you within a spending limit you set? What limit, and what would
   make you trust it?
6. If you paid into a held account that released money only after proof, would you use it? What would stop you?

## After each conversation, write down within the hour

- Exact words for anything you might quote, marked as a quote.
- One line: the most important thing you learned.
- Anything that surprised you or argued against the idea.
